Understanding Uninsured Motorist Insurance

Did You Know? In Illinois, 1 in 8 drivers are uninsured. If one hits you, will you be protected?

Hi there,

As your personal injury attorneys, we see it too often: someone is injured in a crash, the other driver is clearly at fault, but they have no insurance. What should be a straightforward claim becomes a financial nightmare.

That's where Uninsured Motorist (UM) coverage comes in—and why we strongly recommend you understand it.

What Is Uninsured Motorist Coverage?

Uninsured Motorist coverage protects you when you're hit by a driver who has no insurance or not enough insurance to cover your damages. Think of it as insurance for yourself when the other driver can't pay.

Here's a real-world example:

You're stopped at a red light when another driver rear-ends you. You suffer a concussion, miss three weeks of work, and need physical therapy for months. Your medical bills total $75,000.

When you try to file a claim, you discover the other driver has no insurance. Without UM coverage, you're stuck with all those bills. With it, your own insurance steps in to cover what the other driver should have paid.

Your UM coverage essentially becomes the insurance the other driver should have had, protecting you from financial disaster through no fault of your own.

What Does It Cover?

If you're hit by an uninsured or underinsured driver, your UM coverage can pay for:

✓ Medical Expenses

Hospital bills, surgery, physical therapy, future medical care

✓ Lost Wages

Income you couldn't earn while recovering

✓ Pain & Suffering

Compensation for your physical and emotional trauma

✓ Vehicle Damage

Repairs or replacement of your car (with UMPD)

How Much Coverage Should You Have?

Here's our professional recommendation as personal injury attorneys who see these cases every day:

Our Recommendation:

$250,000 / $500,000 minimum

While Illinois requires only $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident, that's rarely enough to cover serious injuries. Medical bills from a significant accident can easily exceed $100,000.

Think about it this way: Your UM coverage should match your lifestyle and assets. If you have a home, savings, or investments, you need enough coverage to protect yourself without draining those resources if you're seriously injured.

The good news? UM coverage is surprisingly affordable—often just $10-30 per month for substantial protection.

What You Should Do Today:

  1. Pull out your auto insurance policy or call your agent
  2. Ask: "What are my current UM/UIM limits?"
  3. If it's less than $250,000/$500,000, ask for a quote to increase it
  4. Compare the small monthly increase vs. the massive protection it provides

We hope you never need to use your UM coverage. But if you do, you'll be grateful you have it.
